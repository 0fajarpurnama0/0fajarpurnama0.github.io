### Prologue: The End and The Cosmic Anomaly

The sky over Silvonia Academy did not burn; it simply dissolved.

Adelle Seris, the tragic saintess, had finally reached the absolute limit of her holy power. After countless regressions, the fabric of reality had frayed until it snapped. There would be no more second chances. The mana streams that birthed spirits and fueled magic dried up into a suffocating vacuum. The world was ending not with a bang, but with a silent, localized erasure.

Sitting amidst the floating, disintegrating rubble of the Ophelius Hall was Ed Rothstaylor. The Korean reincarnator was battered, his clothes torn, his meticulously crafted survival gear reduced to ash. He had tried everything. He had manipulated the scenarios, trained his magic, and pushed his physical limits. Yet, against the total collapse of the timeline itself, his desperate will to survive seemed utterly meaningless.

"So this is a dead end," Ed muttered, his breath crystallizing in the void. He closed his eyes, expecting the final wave of erasure to take him.

"It is, if you look at it strictly from a localized, third-dimensional perspective."

Ed’s eyes snapped open.

Standing a few paces away, floating effortlessly above the void, was a man who clearly did not belong in this universe. He wore a casual but distinctly foreign attire, completely unaffected by the apocalyptic pressure crushing the dimension. But what paralyzed Ed’s survival instincts wasn't the man's clothes—it was his presence.

Ed had seen ancient dragons, high spirits, and archmages. This man felt like something else entirely. It was an aura that transcended mana—a dense, cosmic gravity that reminded Ed of a universe expanding. The man possessed the absolute stillness of someone who had fought gods, crossed multiverses, and touched the precipice of Dragon Ball Super-tier cosmic power.

"Who are you?" Ed asked, his voice steady despite the overwhelming pressure.

"My name is Fajar Purnama," the man smiled, radiating a carefree, friendly warmth that stood in stark contrast to the dying world around them. "I’m just a traveler passing through the infinite realities. I sensed a spatial anomaly—your Saintess’s regression engine completely stalling out—and decided to take a look."

Fajar stepped forward, the dissolving reality solidifying beneath his feet. "You have an impressive will to survive, Ed Rothstaylor. I’ve seen countless souls give up long before the world actually ends. You’re still calculating how to breathe in a vacuum."

"Survival is a habit," Ed replied, keeping his guard up. "But unless you have a way to fix a shattered timeline, my habits won't matter much."

Fajar chuckled, looking up at the fracturing sky. "I can fix it. But I don't usually interfere directly with my full strength. It ruins the local balance, and frankly, waving a hand to instantly solve a universe's problems gets a bit boring when you've reached my phase."

Fajar looked back at Ed, his eyes carrying the weight of eons yet the spark of a new adventure. "I have a proposition. We reset this timeline. You go back to the beginning, armed with the knowledge of this failure. To help you, I will send a soul fragment of myself to accompany you. This fragment will start as a normal, powerless human. Ed processed the information with terrifying speed. A reset. A second chance. A companion who possessed the mind of a multiversal deity but the body of a mortal, ensuring the timeline wouldn't be instantly derailed by overwhelming cosmic force.

"What is your fragment's objective?" Ed asked, his pragmatic nature taking over.

"Simple," Fajar replied, extending a hand. "Survive. Save yourself, save the people of this academy, and save the world from reaching this dead end again. No complex coexistence politics, no grand religious crusades. Just pure, unadulterated survival and progression."

Ed looked at the hand. The void was inches away from consuming them.

"Deal." Ed grasped Fajar's hand.

Fajar grinned. His cosmic ki flared—a brilliant, blinding light that swallowed the darkness, bypassing the laws of mana entirely and forcing the gears of the universe to violently rewind.

### Chapter 1.2: The Groundskeeper’s Qi

By the third day, the camp was functional. They had a waterproof shelter, a smoking rack for fish, and a perimeter of basic snare traps. But survival required capital, and capital required access to the Academy.

He stood before the massive trunk, holding a standard-issue iron wood-axe. Several combat students in crisp uniforms walked past, casting disdainful glances at the common laborer. Fajar ignored them. He closed his eyes, sinking into his stance.

*Breathe in. Draw the ambient energy.*

Silvonia was rich in mana. To the students, mana was a tool to be commanded—shaped into fireballs or wind blades. But Fajar didn't command it. He *inhaled* it. He let the dense, external mana of the forest flow into his lungs, mixing it with the internal biological Qi generated by his heartbeat.

His muscles subtly tightened. His blood ran hot. He wasn't casting a spell; he was using the world's mana to forcefully overclock his physical vessel.

*Exhale.*

Fajar swung the axe.

*CRACK.*

The iron blade sheared through the three-foot-thick oak trunk in a single, explosive kinetic strike. The shockwave rattled the leaves of nearby trees. The two halves of the massive log hit the grass with a heavy thud.

A few yards away, a group of combat students stopped dead in their tracks, staring in absolute bewilderment at the smiling groundskeeper who had just cleaved a boulder-sized tree with zero incantations and a cheap iron tool.

"Excellent," Fajar muttered to himself, hoisting the massive logs onto his cart. "The Qi-Mana synthesis is highly efficient. Ed will be pleased. Free firewood."

### Chapter 1.3: The First Visitor

### Chapter 1.5: The Hierarchy of Defense

The next morning, before Fajar had to report for his groundskeeping duties, the clearing became a sparring ring.

Fajar stood barefoot in the grass, his hands resting easily at his sides. Ed stood a few paces away, holding a padded wooden training knife he had carved the night before.

"Usually," Fajar began, stretching his neck, "when I teach the basics of combat to normal people—like a couple of middle-school kids I trained back in a world filled with Yokai—I have to start by breaking their delusions. I have to teach them the ugly reality of street fighting."

Fajar pointed at Ed. "But you? You're different. I watched you fight in a dying timeline. Your pragmatism, your strategic mindset, and your sheer ruthlessness in realistic combat are already top-tier. You know how to fight dirty. You just lack the physical framework to maximize it."

Ed tightened his grip on the wooden knife. "So where do we start?"

"We review the foundation of survival. The Hierarchy of Defense," Fajar explained, holding up his hand and counting off his fingers.

"**Number One:** Escaping and utilizing the environment. This is your specialty. In a real battle, the best defense is not being in the fight at all, or forcing the enemy to fight the terrain instead of you. Never lose this instinct."

"**Number Two:** Avoiding. If you can't escape the arena, simply don't be where the attack lands. It requires spatial awareness. You move your body out of the trajectory before the strike is even fully launched."

"**Number Three:** Dodging. This is reactive. Evading at the last possible second. It expends more energy than avoiding, but it's necessary when fighting high-speed opponents or magic projectiles."

"**Number Four:** Blocking and Hardening. When you can't dodge, you brace. You use your neuro-somatic cultivation to harden your muscles, bones, and joints to take the impact safely. You sacrifice a limb's mobility temporarily to protect your vitals."

Fajar finally held up his thumb. "**Number Five:** Parrying. The absolute pinnacle of defense. It requires perfect timing and supreme accuracy. You don't stop the enemy's force; you redirect it. A perfect parry completely shatters the opponent's posture, opening them up for a lethal counter. It is the most dangerous to attempt, but the most rewarding."

Fajar beckoned to Ed with two fingers. "Come at me. Use your survival pragmatism. Try to stab me, and I will show you the difference between avoiding and parrying."

Ed didn't hesitate. He burst forward, his boots kicking up dirt. He didn't aim high like a novice; he feinted a slash to Fajar's chest, immediately dropping his weight to drive the wooden knife toward Fajar's thigh—a classic survivalist crippling strike.

Fajar didn't step back. He didn't even widen his eyes.

*Avoid.* Fajar simply shifted his hips two inches to the right. Ed's blade sliced through empty air, throwing his balance forward by a fraction of a millimeter.

*Parry.* Fajar’s hand snapped out like a viper. He didn't grab Ed's wrist; he simply slapped the back of Ed's hand with the exact amount of kinetic force needed.

The redirect was flawless. Ed's own momentum betrayed him. The tiny slap sent Ed spiraling forward, his feet tangling as he crashed face-first into the soft mud of the clearing.

Ed groaned, spitting out a mouthful of dirt.

"Perfect timing, perfect accuracy," Fajar said cheerfully, offering Ed a hand up. "Your intent to kill was excellent, Ed. But your physical structure betrayed your attack vector. Once we align your somatic pathways, you'll be the one redirecting the force. Let's go again."

Ed wiped the mud from his chin, a sharp, competitive grin forming on his face. This wasn't just survival anymore. This was absolute evolution.

"Again," Ed agreed, dropping back into his stance.
